How this estimate works

We calculate consumed volume from bottle volume × the fraction you drink, or shot size × shot count. Both modes use the bottle’s ABV. Pure alcohol equals consumed volume × ABV × 0.789 grams per mL. A US standard drink contains 14 grams of alcohol.

The simplified Widmark model divides that alcohol by body weight and a distribution factor, then subtracts 0.015 percentage points per hour. We use factors of 0.68 and 0.55 to show two common model assumptions instead of asking for sex. This is a sensitivity range, not a confidence interval or an upper limit.

The model assumes the selected alcohol has been absorbed and you started with no alcohol in your system. It does not model each sip or delayed absorption. Food, body composition, age, health, medications, drinking speed, and individual metabolism change the result. BAC can keep rising after your last drink. These adult population assumptions are not suitable for children.

There are 21 approximate impairment bands, from “Little noticeable effect” through “Buzzed,” “Lightly tipsy,” “Drunk,” “Clearly drunk,” “Heavily drunk,” “Blackout risk,” and “Extreme intoxication” to “Critical poisoning risk.” A separate label applies when the selected portion contains no alcohol. The labels are educational shorthand, not medical categories or predictions. The finer cutoffs are UI choices, not validated biological thresholds; blackouts and overdose can happen at lower levels.

Sources: Widmark model and alcohol density, time adjustment assumptions, BAC and impairment, and blackouts.

Know the danger signs.

If someone can’t be woken, has slow or irregular breathing, or has a seizure, call emergency services immediately (911 in the US). Stay with them. Don’t wait for a calculator result.
